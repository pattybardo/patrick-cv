## The Work

I joined Zenseact on the team that owns the Bazel and CI ecosystem for a big monorepo building the autonomous-driving stack. The job was mostly finding the things that were actually slowing people down day to day and fixing them, rather than designing anything grand.

## Testing Changes You Can't Test Before They Land

Some changes can't be validated before they merge. If you're upgrading Bazel itself, your CI runs on the old version — there's nothing to test the new one against until it's already in. Pre-merge coverage just doesn't exist for that case.

So instead of trying to force it, I set up a post-merge validation pattern on a branch: a designated branch tracks the environmental change, and CI validates against it after merge but before it reaches the wider fleet. I first used it for a Bazel version bump, but it works for any change where the thing you'd test with is the thing you're changing.

## Dark Builds

We depended on a pre-built baseline from our parent company that Zenseact's software sat on top of. When an engineer needed to test a patch against a different version of that baseline — usually to prove a fix in the parent's code actually resolved a bug we were seeing — the only way to do it was locally. Ad-hoc rebuilds, uncommitted patches, results nobody else could see or reproduce.

I built a CLI-driven dark build workflow that pulled this into the normal flow. You trigger a baseline rebuild with a command, it runs through CI on a branch, and the patches get committed and reviewed like anything else. Same work, but now there's a trail.

## BuildBuddy

I stood up a self-hosted BuildBuddy instance so developers could see where time was actually going in their Bazel builds in CI. It was basically a Friday afternoon of work, but having an org-wide view of build timing was genuinely useful — both for picking what was worth optimising and for explaining to engineers why a particular change of theirs was slow.
