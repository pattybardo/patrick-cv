## Context

Woodbine Entertainment is one of Canada's largest horse racing companies, operating major tracks in Ontario. The engineering challenge was a data one: enormous volumes of historical and live wagering data, loosely structured, with the goal of understanding bettor behaviour well enough to surface better experiences in real time.

## Big Data Pipeline

The core of the work was wrangling wagering data at scale using the Hadoop ecosystem — **Hive** for SQL-style analysis over HDFS, and **PySpark** for more complex transformations that required the full expressiveness of a distributed compute framework.

Converted loosely defined business questions — "which bettors are underperforming their stated strategy?" — into concrete analytical pipelines. The process involved significant data cleaning, joins across multiple source systems, and building reusable transformation libraries that the team could compose rather than rewriting from scratch.

## Probabilistic Models

Built and optimised probabilistic models designed to surface better wagering opportunities for customers. The modelling work involved understanding the statistical structure of pari-mutuel betting markets and identifying where the implied probabilities diverged from what historical data suggested.

Used **CUDA programming** for Monte Carlo simulations to verify model results — running millions of simulated race outcomes to validate that the models behaved correctly under a range of conditions rather than just the historical average.

## Real-Time Delivery

The analytical work fed into a real-time optimisation layer: algorithms that could take model outputs and surface smart wagering suggestions to thousands of customers simultaneously. Built **Spring Boot microservices** to handle customer onboarding and serve recommendations, connecting the batch analytical work to the live product.

## Data Integration

Developed Python scripts to automate **Sqoop** data integration — moving data from relational databases into the Hadoop ecosystem reliably and repeatably, so the analytical pipelines always had fresh inputs.
