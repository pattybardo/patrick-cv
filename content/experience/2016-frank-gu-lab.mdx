## The Work

The Frank Gu Lab at the University of Waterloo focuses on nanotechnology and advanced materials. As an undergraduate researcher, I worked independently on the synthesis and characterisation of mesoporous carbon — a material with applications in energy storage and filtration.

The work was less about the chemistry and more about the scientific rigour required to produce trustworthy results.

## Experimental Design

Designed experimental protocols from first principles: varying synthesis parameters systematically, controlling for confounds, and structuring experiments so that results were interpretable and reproducible. Good experimental design in a lab context is the same discipline as good A/B testing in a product context — the goal is to isolate the variable you care about and measure its effect cleanly.

Developed **scale-up synthesis protocols** for mesoporous carbon — translating small-batch laboratory procedures into repeatable larger-scale processes, where new challenges around heat distribution, reaction uniformity, and yield consistency emerged.

## Data Analysis and Paper Analysis

A significant part of independent research is reading the literature critically — not just absorbing what papers report, but understanding how their experimental designs constrain the conclusions they can draw, and where the gaps are. Developed the habit of interrogating papers at the methodological level: what assumptions are baked in, what controls were run, what would invalidate this result?

Applied quantitative analysis to characterisation data — BET surface area measurements, TEM imaging analysis, pore size distribution curves — to draw conclusions about synthesis outcomes and inform the next experimental iteration.

## What This Built

The instinct to ask "how do we know this is true?" before "what does this tell us?" came from research. It applies directly to engineering: the same scepticism that makes a good experimentalist makes a good systems engineer — especially when debugging distributed systems or evaluating whether an observability signal actually reflects reality.
