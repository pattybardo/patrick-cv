## What I Did

Joined as the fifth engineer and first dedicated platform hire. The company had grown to 80 engineers across 12 teams, all fighting the same battles: flaky CI, inconsistent environments, and deployments that required three Slack threads and a prayer.

I built the Internal Developer Platform (IDP) that changed that.

## The Platform

The core was a control plane service in Go, backed by a Postgres state store and a Redis-based event bus. Engineers describe their services via a YAML manifest — dependencies, resource requirements, scaling policy, SLOs — and the platform handles everything downstream: provisioning, secrets injection, network policy, observability wiring.

```yaml
service:
  name: payments-api
  replicas:
    min: 2
    max: 20
    metric: rps_per_pod
  dependencies:
    - postgres:payments
    - redis:session-cache
  slo:
    p99_latency_ms: 150
    error_rate_percent: 0.1
```

## Impact

- Reduced mean deployment time from **40 minutes** to **3.8 minutes**
- Cut on-call pages related to infrastructure drift by **73%**
- Onboarded 12 teams in 6 weeks with zero migration downtime
- The IDP now processes ~2,000 deployments per week

## What I Learned

The hardest problem wasn't the technology. It was the **contract negotiation** — agreeing on what the platform owns vs. what teams own. We drew that line too late the first time, had to redraw it, and the second time we got it right by involving team leads in the design process early.

The lesson: a platform team without a product mindset is just another team that makes everyone else slow.
