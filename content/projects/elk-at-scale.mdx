## Scale

Meltwater's ELK stack handles the log and event output of a large distributed product used by thousands of customers. At peak it indexes over **a billion documents a day**. At that volume the things you can get away with skipping on a small cluster stop being optional.

## Index Lifecycle Management

First job was moving to **ILM (Index Lifecycle Management)** for rollover and retention. Before that, index management was a mix of manual steps and fragile cron jobs. With ILM in place, indices roll over on a size or age threshold, move down to warm and cold tiers as they age, and get deleted when their retention window is up. That cleared out a whole category of manual work and made retention consistent and easy to reason about.

## Shard Strategy

At this scale, where shards land has a real effect on query performance and on whether the cluster stays upright. I worked on the number of shards per index, shard sizing, and rebalancing so we didn't end up with hot shards — one shard taking disproportionate load because a customer's data happens to be concentrated in a narrow slice of time.

## Per-Tenant Routing

I built a per-tenant routing layer in the indexing pipeline so high-volume customers get routed to dedicated index paths instead of dominating shared infrastructure. It isolated the noisy tenants without needing separate hardware for each of them.

## The Version Migration

The foundation under all of this was the migration from Elasticsearch 1.7 to 7.x — six major versions, incompatible on-disk formats, and a live cluster that couldn't go offline. The approach (shadow indexing, backfill, a query compatibility shim, and a metered cut-over) is written up in the Meltwater Software Engineer role.
