## Scale

Meltwater's ELK stack processes the log and event output of a large distributed product, used by thousands of customers daily. At peak, the pipeline indexes over **a billion documents per day** — a scale where the operational decisions that are optional at smaller volumes become mandatory.

## Index Lifecycle Management

The first major improvement was introducing **ILM (Index Lifecycle Management)** for automated rollover and retention. Before ILM, index management was a mix of manual operations and fragile cron jobs. With ILM, indices automatically roll over when they hit a size or age threshold, move to warm/cold tiers as they age, and are deleted when their retention window expires.

## Shard Strategy

At this scale, shard allocation strategy has a significant impact on query performance and cluster stability. Worked on tuning the number of shards per index, shard sizing targets, and rebalancing policies to avoid hot shards — situations where one shard receives disproportionate query or indexing load because a customer's data is concentrated in a narrow time range.

## Per-Tenant Routing

Built a per-tenant routing layer in the indexing pipeline so that high-volume customers (ingesting at a rate that would otherwise dominate shared infrastructure) are routed to dedicated index paths. This isolated noisy tenants without requiring separate physical infrastructure for each one.

## The Version Migration

Migrating the cluster from Elasticsearch 1.7 to 7.x was the foundational project that preceded all of this. Six major versions, incompatible data formats, a live production cluster that couldn't simply be taken offline. The approach — shadow indexing, historical backfill, a query compatibility shim, and a metered traffic cut-over — is documented in the Meltwater Software Engineer role.
